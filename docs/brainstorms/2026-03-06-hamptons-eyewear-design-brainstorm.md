## What We're Building

A luxury sunglasses brand called **Hamptons** targeting the wealthy East Coast demographic, starting in the Hamptons, NY. One hero frame called **"The Classics"** at **$450 retail**. Positioned to look like Tom Ford quality but play in the accessible luxury space between Moscot/Garrett Leight ($340-$455) and Tom Ford ($440-$600).

The brand uses an **HH monogram** and **windmill motif** (the iconic Hamptons symbol). Think Polo Ralph Lauren heritage meets Chrome Hearts packaging meets Tom Ford frame design.

## Key Design Decisions (LOCKED)

### The Frame: "The Classics"

| Spec | Decision |
|------|----------|
| **Shape** | Bold flat-top wayfarer |
| **Colorway** | All black (single colorway at launch) |
| **Acetate** | Mazzucchelli Italian acetate (est. 1849, the gold standard) |
| **Lenses** | Carl Zeiss polarized glass, dark smoke/black tint |
| **Metal accents** | .925 sterling silver, rhodium coated (no tarnish, bright cool finish) |
| **Signature detail** | H-shaped hinge hardware (the H IS the hinge, like Tom Ford's T) |
| **Hinges** | 5-barrel spring hinges (OBE German-engineered preferred) |
| **Nose pads** | Integrated acetate (no adjustable pads, cleaner wayfarer look) |
| **Inside temple text** | "HAMPTONS NEW YORK" + model/size numbers |
| **External branding** | H-hinge only, nothing else on the frame |
| **Retail price** | $450 |

### Branding Approach: Quiet Luxury

- **On the frame:** Only the sterling silver H-shaped hinge. Small (6-8mm). Engraved, not stamped. You have to look twice to notice it.
- **Inside temple:** "HAMPTONS NEW YORK" in small engraved text + "The Classics" + sizing (e.g., 54-18-145)
- **The windmill crest** lives on packaging, marketing, and cases only. NOT on the frame.
- **The HH circle monogram** is the brand mark for digital/print. NOT on the frame.
- **Philosophy:** Bottega Veneta "no logo" approach. Quality speaks. Insiders know.

### Packaging: Chrome Hearts Level

| Component | Spec |
|-----------|------|
| **Outer box** | Rigid magnetic-close, matte black, gold foil "HAMPTONS" wordmark + windmill crest |
| **Case** | Genuine leather (black), debossed HH monogram, metal snap or zipper closure |
| **Cloth** | Microfiber with tonal HH pattern |
| **Insert** | Authenticity card + care card |
| **Experience** | The leather case IS something people carry daily. Walking advertisement. |

## Why This Approach

### Competitive Positioning

| Brand | Price | Our advantage |
|-------|-------|---------------|
| Tom Ford | $440-600 | We match their entry point with comparable materials + Hamptons identity |
| Oliver Peoples | $380-530 | Same tier, but we have Hamptons geographic identity |
| Garrett Leight | $355-455 | They make a "Hampton" model, we OWN the name |
| Moscot | $340-370 | They just opened in Southampton. We're positioned just above. |
| Krewe | $295-415 | They own New Orleans. We own the Hamptons. |
| Johnny Hampton | Sub-$300 | They exist but DTC only, no retail, no sterling silver, lower tier |
| Ray-Ban | $170-299 | Completely different market. We're the upgrade. |

**Critical insight:** Every successful indie eyewear brand has a geographic/cultural identity. Warby Parker = NYC intellectual. Krewe = New Orleans. Moscot = Lower East Side. Jacques Marie Mage = LA/Japan. **We are the Hamptons.** The windmill IS the Hamptons.

### What Makes $395 Worth It

**Functional differentiators (vs. $150 glasses):**
- Mazzucchelli Italian acetate (richer color, better aging, hypoallergenic)
- Zeiss polarized GLASS lenses (not plastic, better optical clarity)
- Sterling silver hardware (real precious metal)
- 5-barrel spring hinges (smoother action, longer life)
- Hand-polished finishing

**Psychological differentiators (the real reason people pay):**
1. Identity signaling: "I'm the kind of person who wears Hamptons, not Ray-Ban"
2. Tactile quality: the weight, the hinge click, the acetate feel
3. Peer recognition: the H-hinge becomes recognizable in affluent circles
4. The case: genuine leather case people carry daily
5. Scarcity: not in every Sunglass Hut

## Manufacturing Plan

### The Reality of "Made in NYC"

- **Lowercase (Brooklyn)** is the only confirmed company doing full acetate frame production in NYC. They invested $100K+ in Italian CNC machines.
- **Moscot** designs in NYC, manufactures in their family-owned China factory. This is the more common model.
- **Most luxury brands** (Illesteva, Warby Parker) design in NYC, manufacture in Italy or China.

### Recommended Approach

**"Designed and Assembled in New York City"**
- Import Mazzucchelli acetate sheets from Italy
- Have sterling silver H-hinge components made by a NYC jeweler/metalsmith
- Source Zeiss lenses from their B2B division
- Final assembly + hand-finishing in a Brooklyn workshop
- This is honest and carries cache for the Hamptons market

**NYC Contacts to Explore:**
- Lowercase NYC (Brooklyn Army Terminal, Sunset Park) - may not do contract manufacturing (acquired by MODO)
- Match Eyewear (Long Island) - potential contract work
- Charmant USA (Morris Plains, NJ) - produces optical frames
- Maker's Row / ThomasNet directories for custom manufacturers
- NYC jewelry manufacturers for sterling silver hinge components

## Competitive Threat: Johnny Hampton

A brand called "Johnny Hampton" already sells Hamptons-themed sunglasses:
- Models named "Southampton," "East Hampton," "Bridgehampton," "Shelter Island"
- Italian Mazzucchelli acetate, polarized CR-39 lenses, stainless steel
- Priced below $300
- DTC only (johnnyhampton.com), no retail presence
- 5% charity donation

**Our advantages over Johnny Hampton:**
1. Sterling silver hardware (they use stainless steel)
2. Zeiss glass lenses (they use CR-39 plastic)
3. Physical retail + events strategy (they're DTC only)
4. Stronger brand identity (windmill crest, H-hinge vs. no distinctive hardware)
5. Higher price point = higher perceived value
6. Chrome Hearts-level packaging (leather case vs. standard)
